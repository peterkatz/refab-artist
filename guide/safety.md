# Safety

A cut file moves a real machine, and a laser or a router can hurt you or start a fire. This page is general good practice, not legal or professional advice. Your machine's manual, your makerspace's rules and the safety data for your materials come first.

## What Refab Artist checks, and what it doesn't

Before a file reaches you, Refab Artist checks that it is consistent and cuttable: parts fit the sheet, cuts are closed shapes, slots are the right width. That is not the same as safe. It cannot see your machine, your material, your settings, the space around you or where the emergency stop is. Those are yours.

## Materials

!!! danger "Never cut PVC, vinyl or unknown plastics"
    Cutting PVC (polyvinyl chloride), vinyl, and many coated or imitation-leather materials with a laser releases chlorine gas and hydrochloric acid, which are toxic and destroy the machine. If you do not know exactly what a material is, do not cut it. Check its label or ask the supplier for the safety data sheet.

- Use materials sold for your kind of machine. Plywood, MDF, solid wood, cardboard and cast acrylic are common for laser cutting; check that the glue in plywood and MDF is one your machine and extraction can handle.
- Some plastics, such as polycarbonate and some foams, burn badly or give off harmful fumes. Leave them alone unless your machine's maker says they are safe.
- Cutting produces fine dust and fumes, including from wood and MDF. Treat them as something to extract, not breathe.

## Laser cutters

- **Ventilation and extraction.** Run the extraction (or vent outdoors) every time, and keep the filters in good order.
- **Never leave a running laser.** Stay with the machine until the job is done. Cutting wood, card and acrylic can ignite, and a small flame can become a fire in seconds.
- **Fire.** Keep a suitable fire extinguisher within reach and know how to use it. Keep the bed and the area underneath clean, because offcuts and dust burn.
- **Eye protection.** Keep the lid closed while the laser is on. If you must work with the lid open or the machine's guards removed, wear eyewear rated for your laser's wavelength; ordinary glasses and sunglasses are not enough.
- **Test your settings** on scrap first, and use the lowest power and passes that cut cleanly.

## CNC routers

- Wear eye and ear protection, and a dust mask or use dust extraction.
- Clamp the sheet down firmly. Keep hands, hair, sleeves and jewellery away from the spindle.
- Know where the emergency stop is. Do not leave the machine running.
- Use the right bit for the material and the right speeds and feeds for your machine.

## Cutting by hand

- Use a sharp blade and a steel ruler, cut away from your body, and use a cutting mat.
- Cut in several shallow passes rather than one deep one.

## Before every job

- Check the file's scale and that you have chosen the right material.
- Look the sheet over in the cut-sheet preview and read the Issues tab.
- Run a small test first, as described in [Cutting and fit](cutting-and-fit.md).
- Keep the emergency stop within reach, and stay with the machine.
